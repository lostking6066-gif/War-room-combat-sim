If you paste a URL but no transcript, the game will say so plainly: no video was watched and no transcript was accessed, and the battle continues in ordinary research mode. Death Rules — what counts as a win

Victory is by death (HP reaches 0), or by incapacitation lasting the rest of the fight, or by battlefield removal with no way back. The exact victory condition is stated in the results and in Chapter VI.

Regeneration can heal, but it does NOT revive after HP 0. A fighter who is knocked out stays knocked out — no dead fighter acts again, and regeneration only heals a fighter who is still alive.

No invented killing blow: if the battle hits its round cap with both fighters still alive and neither able to kill or incapacitate the other, the result is a stalemate / no-death verdict, explained in Chapter VI — not a fake finish.

Battlefield removal: removal alone does not count unless the opponent cannot return. No power in the current powersets permanently removes an opponent from the battlefield, so v12 will not claim a BFR victory it did not simulate.

Online Storytelling Pipeline — Research → Combat → Narration → Check → Save

Stage 1 — Research Dossier: load each fighter's structured profile, then fetch Wikipedia (encyclopedia) and fan-run Fandom wiki sources automatically. Fan sources and YouTube searches are discussion to investigate, not established fact, and never auto-grant powers.
Stage 2 — Matchup Analysis: compare power, speed, dexterity, durability, stamina, tactics, transformations, weaknesses and win conditions for the selected continuities only — versions are never silently merged.
Stage 3 — Combat Simulation: our own engine decides the battle and records every exchange. The internet is evidence; the engine is the outcome.
Stage 4 — Episode: the narrator turns the battle record into 6 chapters, with Analytical, Cinematic and Reflective voices.
Stage 5 — Consistency Check: the episode is checked against the record before it is presented as verified.
Stage 6 — Saved Episode: matchup, continuities, outcome, research references and the full episode are saved on this device only, so you can re-read them.
Free storytelling, honestly: the rule-based narrator above is the default and is free. The optional Pollinations / Gemini draft is a separate, labelled draft and never replaces the verified episode. Free services can impose quotas, change availability, or require accounts or keys — internet access alone does not provide a language model, and this game is not built around an assumed free LLM. Automatic YouTube transcript reading still needs a YouTube Data API key; the game uses YouTube search links and your pasted notes instead.

WAR-ROOM AI — Four Connected Brains for 1v1 (rule-based, no paid API)

1. Combat Brain: tier scaling, counters, stamina, battlefield destruction, transformations at or below 45% HP — a street-level fighter cannot routinely hurt a cosmic foe by luck alone.
2. Character Brain: personality decisions — Batman studies first, Goku holds back to test a foe, Vegeta's pride seeks the strongest opponent, humorous fighters are unpredictable. In 1v1 there is no ally to protect, so protective shielding cannot trigger.
3. Knowledge Brain + Analysis Brain: identity, continuity, powers, feats, limits, weaknesses, transformations, relationships — plus Wikipedia + Fandom research, shown per source — and a head-to-head comparison of strength, speed, durability, abilities, experience, transformations, weaknesses and win conditions, with an explicit uncertainty note when a matchup is close.
4. Narrator Brain: turns the analysis and the recorded events into a 6-chapter episode (Contenders, Comparison, First Exchange, Turning Point, Final Clash, Verdict), using tactics and reasons, not just attack names.
Continuity check (adapted to 1v1): checks the battle before the episode is shown — a dead or incapacitated fighter must not act, powers must belong to that version, transformations must meet their trigger, and the winner must match the survivor / stalemate record.

Trade-off, stated plainly: this is a sophisticated rule system, not a language model. It can calculate, decide, and narrate from rules reliably, but it cannot write unlimited original prose like an LLM. That last step would need a local open model or a free/paid inference service, which v12 does not require.

This version connects, from the browser, to: Wikipedia, fan-run Fandom wikis, YouTube search, and — optionally — a free public AI text service (Pollinations, no key) or your own free Gemini key. Security note: a key in a browser game can be seen in network traffic on that device. Use a free key, never a paid/production key, and rotate it if you share the device.
